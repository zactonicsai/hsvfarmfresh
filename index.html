Fall recipes, cooked and raw

Raw, baked, or lightly cooked. Each way of cooking has something going for it: raw keeps all the vitamin C, roasting brings out sweetness, and a quick steam or sauté keeps vegetables bright with little effort. Apples come from local orchards this time of year; bananas and mangoes don't grow in North Alabama, so pick those up at the grocery store. Every raw recipe is vegan and uses six ingredients or fewer.

    Grow your own kale, starting with one plant

    Kale is the easiest green to grow through a North Alabama fall and winter. Skip the seeds: a starter plant from the garden center at Home Depot or Lowe's gets you picking leaves in a few weeks.

    Why kale earns a spot

      Taking a blood thinner such as warfarin? Vitamin K affects how it works. You don't have to avoid kale, but keep the amount you eat steady from week to week and check with your doctor or pharmacist.
